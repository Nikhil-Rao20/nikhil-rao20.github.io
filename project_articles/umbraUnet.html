Abstract

Semantic segmentation of satellite imagery is a cornerstone of modern Earth Observation, yet its accuracy is frequently compromised by severe class imbalance, where critical but rare classes like small water bodies or deforested patches are overshadowed by dominant labels like vegetation. Standard deep learning pipelines, often relying on generic loss functions and optimizers, tend to achieve high overall accuracy at the cost of poor recall on these minority classes. To address this fundamental challenge, we introduce a novel, end-to-end framework engineered specifically for imbalanced remote sensing data.

At the core of our solution is the Umbra UNet, a cutting-edge hybrid deep learning architecture that integrates state-of-the-art vision blocks—InceptionNeXt, EfficientViT, and ConvNeXtV2—to achieve a superior fusion of local and global features. To complement this architectural innovation, we developed a hybrid "Combo" loss function, a weighted blend of Focal, Dice, and Tversky losses, designed to force the model's attention onto underrepresented classes. Finally, we couple this with a dataset-aware AdamW optimizer for enhanced training stability and generalization.

Validated on the challenging DLRSD benchmark, our integrated approach demonstrates a marked improvement in segmenting minority classes compared to baseline methods, offering a robust, stable, and highly accurate solution for real-world geospatial analysis.

1. Introduction

The proliferation of high-resolution satellite imagery from missions like ISRO's LISS-IV has unlocked unprecedented opportunities for monitoring our planet's surface. From tracking deforestation and managing water resources to urban planning and disaster response, the ability to automatically generate accurate land cover maps through semantic segmentation is of paramount importance. Deep learning, particularly with architectures like the U-Net [1], has become the state-of-the-art for this task. However, the performance of these models in real-world scenarios is often hindered by a persistent and fundamental problem: class imbalance.

In nearly all remote sensing datasets, the distribution of land cover classes is heavily skewed. Vast expanses of vegetation, urban areas, or bare soil (the majority classes) dominate the landscape, while smaller, yet often more critical, features like wetlands, isolated water bodies, or new construction sites (the minority or "weaker" classes) are vastly underrepresented. This imbalance poses a significant challenge to standard training paradigms.

The Class Imbalance Challenge

Deep learning models trained with conventional loss functions, such as cross-entropy, and generic optimizers like Adam are mathematically driven to maximize overall accuracy. This inadvertently biases the model towards the majority classes, as correctly classifying a pixel of vegetation has a much larger impact on the total loss than correctly classifying a rare pixel of a wetland. Consequently, these models often learn to ignore the minority classes entirely, resulting in high overall accuracy but dangerously poor recall on the very targets that are often of greatest interest.

This project, undertaken for the ISRO Bharatiya Antariksh Hackathon 2025, directly confronts this problem statement. The objective was to move beyond incremental improvements and devise a mathematically motivated, holistic framework to explicitly improve the segmentation of weaker classes in satellite imagery. Our contribution is a comprehensive, three-pronged solution that innovates across the entire deep learning pipeline: a novel model architecture tailored for the task, a custom loss function to redirect the learning process, and an optimized training strategy to ensure robust convergence.

2. A Multi-Faceted Strategy for Imbalanced Data

Our approach was to engineer a synergistic system where each component is specifically designed to counteract the effects of class imbalance. The process flow, from data to metrics, represents our end-to-end strategy for addressing this fundamental challenge in remote sensing.

End-to-End Strategy

We begin with the DLRSD dataset, which exhibits the characteristic class imbalance seen in real-world remote sensing data. We then propose our novel Umbra UNet as a superior alternative to the baseline UNet. This is coupled with our Combo Loss, which is designed to outperform standard Weighted Cross-Entropy. Finally, we employ optimized training strategies to produce accurate segmentation maps, which are evaluated using metrics that prioritize per-class performance like Dice and IoU.

This integrated framework addresses class imbalance through three synergistic innovations:

Novel Architecture

The Umbra UNet leverages hybrid vision blocks to capture both local textures and global spatial context, essential for identifying small, dispersed minority class regions in satellite imagery.

Targeted Loss Function

Our Combo Loss strategically combines Focal, Dice, and Tversky losses to force model attention onto underrepresented classes while maintaining overall segmentation quality.

Optimized Training

Dataset-aware AdamW optimization provides enhanced training stability and improved generalization for the challenging imbalanced data distribution.

2.1. A Novel Hybrid Architecture: The Umbra UNet

Standard U-Net architectures, while effective, can sometimes struggle to capture the complex interplay of local textures and global spatial context inherent in high-resolution satellite imagery. To address this, we developed the Umbra UNet, a cutting-edge hybrid deep learning architecture meticulously designed for this domain.

Figure 2: Architecture of the proposed solution: UMBRA UNet Framework showing the hybrid encoder design with parallel vision blocks and attention mechanisms.

The Umbra Conv Block Innovation

The primary innovation of the Umbra UNet lies in its encoder's fundamental building block: the Umbra Conv block. This is a powerful hybrid module that processes an input tensor through three parallel branches, each leveraging a different state-of-the-art architectural philosophy to create a multi-faceted feature representation.

Parallel Branch Architecture

The Umbra Conv block integrates three cutting-edge vision approaches:

InceptionNeXt Branch

The first branch utilizes InceptionNeXt blocks [2], which are adept at capturing multi-scale spatial features through their use of split-depthwise convolutions. This allows the model to analyze features at different resolutions simultaneously, crucial for detecting objects of varying sizes in satellite imagery.

EfficientViT Branch

The second branch employs an EfficientViT block [3], a lightweight Vision Transformer. This branch is responsible for modeling long-range dependencies and capturing the global context across the image, a crucial capability for understanding large-scale land patterns and spatial relationships.

ConvNeXtV2 Branch

The third branch uses a ConvNeXtV2 block [4], which represents the pinnacle of modern pure-convolutional design, excelling at robust local feature extraction with improved training stability and performance.

Feature Fusion and Integration

The feature maps from these three parallel paths—representing multi-scale local, global, and hierarchical information—are then concatenated. This fused tensor is passed through a final convolutional layer for channel mixing and integration. This entire Umbra Conv block replaces the standard convolutional blocks in the encoder path of a U-Net.

The decoder path then leverages these exceptionally rich, fused features to progressively reconstruct the segmentation map with high fidelity, using specialized Umbra DSD and FuseUp blocks that incorporate attention mechanisms (MSKA and SA) to further refine the output and maintain focus on minority class regions.

2.2. A Custom Hybrid "Combo" Loss Function

The second pillar of our solution is a novel loss function designed to force the model's attention onto the underrepresented classes. Standard cross-entropy loss treats all pixels equally, which is detrimental in an imbalanced setting. Our "Combo Loss" is a carefully weighted sum of three distinct loss functions, each chosen for its specific properties in handling imbalance:

Focal Loss Component

Focal Loss [5]: This loss function modifies the standard cross-entropy loss to down-weight the loss assigned to well-classified examples. This prevents the vast number of easy-to-classify majority class pixels from overwhelming the training signal, allowing the model to focus on the harder, minority class examples.

Dice Loss Component

Dice Loss [6]: Derived from the Dice Similarity Coefficient, this loss directly optimizes for the overlap between the predicted and ground truth segmentation maps. It is inherently robust to class imbalance because it evaluates the union and intersection of regions, treating each class region as a whole entity.

Tversky Loss Component

Tversky Loss [7]: This is a generalization of the Dice loss that introduces two parameters, α and β, to control the weighting of false positives and false negatives. By setting α > β, we can place a higher penalty on false negatives, which is critical for improving the recall of minority classes that the model might otherwise ignore.

Synergistic Combination

By combining these three components, our Combo Loss creates a balanced learning objective that is highly sensitive to the accurate segmentation of rare classes. The weighted combination ensures that the model learns to balance overall accuracy with exceptional performance on minority classes, addressing the core challenge of class imbalance in satellite imagery segmentation.

2.3. Advanced and Stable Optimization

The final component of our framework is the optimization strategy. The choice of optimizer can significantly impact training stability and the model's ability to generalize. After experimenting with several modern optimizers, including Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) [8] and AdaBelief [9], we found that a carefully tuned AdamW [10] optimizer provided the best results.

AdamW Advantages

AdamW improves upon the standard Adam optimizer by decoupling the weight decay from the gradient update, which often leads to better generalization. We adjusted its parameters, such as the learning rate and weight decay, specifically for the DLRSD dataset, resulting in faster convergence and a model that performs more reliably on unseen data.

3. Experiments and Results

We conducted a series of rigorous experiments on the DLRSD dataset to validate each component of our proposed framework. The DLRSD is a challenging public benchmark with 17 distinct land cover classes and a severe class imbalance, making it an ideal testbed for our solution.

3.1. Analysis of Optimizers and Loss Functions

Our initial experiments focused on comparing the effectiveness of different optimizers and loss functions using a baseline UNet model.

Figure 3: Per-class Dice score comparison for the UNet model trained with AdamW, SAM, and AdaBelief optimizers.
Figure 4: Performance comparison of Combo Loss against standard Weighted Cross-Entropy (CE) loss across three model types.

Key Findings from Optimizer Comparison

The results clearly demonstrate the superiority of our tuned AdamW optimizer. It achieved the highest Dice score for nearly every class, and critically, it was the only optimizer that did not completely fail on some of the most challenging minority classes. SAM was competitive but less consistent, while AdaBelief, despite its theoretical advantages, struggled significantly with the imbalanced nature of the data.

Combo Loss Effectiveness

The comparison shows that our Combo Loss provided a dramatic and substantial improvement in the overall Dice score across every single model type tested. This confirms that our custom hybrid loss is highly effective at forcing the models to learn more balanced and accurate representations, directly addressing the core problem of class imbalance.

4. Conclusion

In this project, we successfully developed and validated a comprehensive, multi-faceted framework to address the critical problem of class imbalance in satellite imagery segmentation. We have demonstrated that by innovating across the entire deep learning pipeline—from architecture to loss function to optimization—it is possible to build models that are robust, stable, and highly accurate, particularly in the challenging task of identifying underrepresented classes.

Primary Contributions

  • Umbra UNet Architecture: A cutting-edge hybrid architecture that effectively fuses local and global features through parallel InceptionNeXt, EfficientViT, and ConvNeXtV2 branches.
  • Combo Loss Function: A novel hybrid loss combining Focal, Dice, and Tversky losses specifically designed to address class imbalance in remote sensing data.
  • Optimized Training Strategy: Dataset-aware AdamW optimization providing enhanced stability and generalization for imbalanced data distributions.
  • Comprehensive Validation: Rigorous experimental validation on the challenging DLRSD benchmark demonstrating consistent improvements over baseline methods.

Our primary contribution is the Umbra UNet, a cutting-edge hybrid architecture that effectively fuses local and global features. When combined with our custom Combo Loss and a dataset-aware AdamW optimizer, this framework provides a powerful and scalable solution. Our thorough validation on the DLRSD dataset confirms the efficacy of our approach, which consistently outperforms standard techniques.

Real-World Impact

This work provides a robust and computationally efficient prototype that is well-suited for real-world deployment in the next generation of high-resolution remote sensing tasks. The framework offers a clear path toward more equitable and accurate automated analysis of our planet, with particular strength in identifying critical but underrepresented land cover features that are often of greatest scientific and policy interest.

Future Directions

Future work will focus on extending this framework to multi-temporal analysis, incorporating additional sensor modalities, and developing real-time processing capabilities for operational Earth observation systems. The principles established in this research provide a foundation for addressing class imbalance challenges across diverse remote sensing applications.
